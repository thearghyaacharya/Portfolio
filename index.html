Background

The unusual path
is the point.

I started as a civil engineer. Four years at VIT building things with structure and precision — learning to think in systems, to ask what holds before asking what looks good. Then I walked into marketing and realised the same logic applies: a brand is architecture. It either holds weight or it collapses under it.

At JAGSoM I found the frameworks. In the field at Megamart, Bloom Luxe, and LIMITLESS, I found the reality. The gap between the two is where most marketers get lost. I stay in that gap — running campaigns, talking to customers in stores, reading the data, and then asking why the data says what it says.

I don't separate brand from business. The ₹45 Cr tender I helped secure wasn't won on price — it was won on positioning, perceived quality, and the confidence that comes from knowing your own value. That's brand strategy. It just happened to live in a construction context.

01 Brand Strategy 2024 — 2025

Arvind Fashions — The Brand Upgrade Journey

"The customer who buys Flying Machine today is the Calvin Klein customer in 15 years — if we don't lose them in between."

Built behavioural cohorts from 26,000+ customer records — not just segmentation, but a human upgrade pathway: Flying Machine → Arrow → USPA → Tommy Hilfiger → Calvin Klein. The insight that unlocked it: most fashion brands market at customers. Megamart had the data to market with them, across their entire life stage. Designed Shaadi Fest as a full-lifecycle brand campaign consistent across influencer, digital, and in-store — drove 25% like-for-like growth and 15% improvement in campaign effectiveness.

25% Revenue growth
26K+ Customers mapped
02 Founder · Brand Build 2024 — 2025

LIMITLESS — Bamboo Activewear Built From Real Pain Points

"Everyone was optimising for performance or sustainability. Nobody was doing both in a material that actually breathes."

Founded a sustainable activewear brand from zero. Started with the customer, not the product — deep research into what performance athletes and casual wearers actually couldn't find. Bamboo emerged as the answer to three simultaneous pain points: breathability, sustainability, and softness without sacrificing durability. Built the product line, brand identity, and website around that single insight. Achieved 80 validated sales and won Startup Champions at JAGSoM against all competing teams.

03 Commercial Strategy 2025 — 2026

Acharya Brothers — ₹45 Cr Won on Positioning, Not Price

"The competitor was cheaper. We won anyway. Because we knew the decision-maker wasn't buying construction — they were buying trust."

Government tenders look like pricing exercises. They're actually brand exercises — the question on the table is always: do I trust this company to deliver? Repositioned ABCL's tender approach around quality differentiation and market standing rather than competing on cost. Developed data-backed pricing that reflected the margin we deserved. Secured the ₹45 Cr contract. Also redesigned AA Convention's revenue model — shifted from fixed rental to per-customer pricing, aligning what we charged with the value we actually delivered. Revenue doubled.

04 Performance · D2C 2023 — 2024

Bloom Luxe — 18.66% CTR Because We Knew Exactly Who We Were Talking To

"The creative wasn't better. The audience definition was. We stopped talking to 'women aged 22–35' and started talking to a specific person with a specific frustration."

Co-founded a D2C skincare brand from nothing. The performance marketing success wasn't luck — it came from ruthless audience segmentation before a single ad was written. Built the full analytics stack (GA4, UTM, pixel tracking), ran systematic A/B testing on creative, copy, and audience definitions until the signal was clear. 18.66% CTR against a 3% industry benchmark. The number is real. What it represents: we understood exactly who we were talking to and what they needed to hear before they did.

How I think

Principles that
show up in the work.

These aren't LinkedIn buzzwords. Each one came from a specific failure or a specific realisation in the field.

Data without empathy is just noise.

At Megamart I had 26,000 customer records. The upgrade journey insight didn't come from the spreadsheet — it came from a conversation with a 28-year-old in the Arrow section who said he was "saving up for Tommy." The data confirmed it. The human told me first.

The other P — Purpose — drives everything.

Product, Price, Place, Promotion. I know them all. But a campaign without a reason to exist for the person receiving it is just spending money on attention you haven't earned. Purpose is the brief before the brief.

Attention is rented. Trust is owned.

You can buy reach. You cannot buy the moment someone recommends you to a friend. Every brand decision I make runs through this: does this build something people will actually remember and trust, or does it just perform for today's numbers?

Patience, prayers, progress.

I don't believe in shortcuts. Every startup I built required showing up when there was no evidence it was working yet. The discipline to keep going when nothing is confirming you is the real skill. The results follow the character, not the other way around.
